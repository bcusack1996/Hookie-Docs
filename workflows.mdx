A **workflow** is a project-scoped, multi-step process that starts when a record lands in its _entry dataset_. Steps run in order, share a JSON _context_ scratchpad seeded from the entry record, and can emit events, call AI or an AI agent, call an HTTP endpoint, set and reshape values, branch on a condition, wait for other events, or sleep. You compose pipelines like _lead → AI score → branch on score → notify → wait for approval → follow up_ without running your own queue or state store.

## Build it visually

Open a project's **Workflows** tab and choose **New workflow**, or **Edit** on an existing one. The builder is a canvas: the workflow runs from the **Trigger** at the top, down through its steps.

| Part | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Add a step** | One button per step type. Choosing one adds the step where **Put new steps** says: after the selected step, into a selected branch's `then` or `else`, at the end of the workflow, or unconnected. You can also drag a step type onto the canvas. |
| **Canvas** | Drag steps to arrange them, and drag from a step's handle to another step to connect them. A `branch` has three handles: **next** (bottom) for what runs after the branch, and **then** and **else** (right) for its two arms. Drag the background to pan; hold Ctrl (or ⌘) and scroll, or pinch, to zoom. The minimap shows the whole workflow. |
| **Toolbar** | Undo, redo, duplicate, delete, auto-layout, zoom in, zoom out and fit to screen. |
| **Edit panel** | The selected step's settings, as a form for its type: dataset pickers that list the project's datasets (or take a new name), an AI-agent dropdown, condition rows (path, operator, value), header and mapping rows, and JSON fields for payloads and bodies. **Connections** sets what runs next, or a branch's `then` and `else`, from a list, so you can connect steps without dragging. |
| **Definition (JSON)** | Exactly what **Save** sends: the same definition the API, the CLI and MCP agents read and write. The canvas is a view of that definition, not a second format. |

Everything works from the keyboard. Tab moves through the palette, the toolbar, the steps on the canvas and the edit panel. Enter selects a focused step, and the arrow keys move it. The **Edit** menu jumps to any step. Shortcuts: Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y) redo, Ctrl+D duplicate, and Delete removes the selected steps while the canvas has focus.

Deleting a step from the middle of a chain reconnects the steps on either side of it. Duplicating one step puts the copy straight after the original. Step positions are not saved: opening a workflow, or **Auto-layout**, arranges it from its structure, with branch arms to the right of the branch.

### Checked before you save

The builder checks the workflow as you edit and marks each problem on the step that has it, with a list above the canvas. **Save** stays blocked until there are none. It checks for:

- an entry dataset on the trigger, and at least one step connected to it;
- steps that nothing connects to the trigger (they would never run);
- the step limit (32, counting steps inside branches) and branch nesting (3 levels);
- a `delay` or `wait_for_event` inside a branch;
- each step's own fields, such as a missing or non-https URL, a reserved header, instructions over 8,000 characters, a delay over 365 days, or a branch condition path that is not in the context (see below).

Hookie's API runs the same checks again when you save, so a workflow saved from the API, the CLI or an agent is held to the same rules.

## Runs on the graph

Every workflow's builder has a **Run history** section. Choose a run and each step on the canvas shows what happened to it in that run: completed, running, waiting, failed or skipped. Steps inside a branch show which arm ran. Below the graph, a table lists every step with its start and finish times and any error.

A run that is pending, running or waiting can be stopped with **Cancel run**. A run that failed, was cancelled or timed out can be started again with **Retry run**. Both are also in the **Instances** dialog on the workflow list, which has a **Show on the graph** button for each run.

## Branching

A `branch` step evaluates one or more conditions against the instance context, using the same condition shape used everywhere else in Hookie (see [Conditions](#conditions)), and runs `then` when they match, `else` otherwise. Because conditions read the context, you can branch on _anything an earlier step produced_: score a lead with AI, then route high-scorers one way and everyone else another. Branches nest up to **3 levels deep**, and every taken path is recorded in step history so you can see exactly which way an instance went.

### Where a branch condition's path points

This is the one thing worth getting right before you write a branch, because the two condition fields on a workflow read **different things**:

| Field | Evaluated against | Path to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `entry_conditions` | the raw record | the bare field — `"topic"` (an `entry.` prefix is refused) |
| a `branch` step's `conditions` | the instance context | `"entry.topic"` |

The context is a scratchpad seeded from the entry record, and the record sits under `entry` there. Alongside it are the keys earlier steps wrote: `call_ai` puts its reply under its `output_key` (default `ai_result`), `agent_call` under `agent_result`, `http` under `http_result`, `transform` under `transformed`, `set` under each key it sets, and `wait_for_event` under `waited_event`. So `"entry.tier"` reads the incoming lead, and `"score"` reads what the AI step just produced — as in the example below.

<Warning>
  Writing a branch condition as `"topic"` when you mean `"entry.topic"` used to be silent: the path resolved to nothing, the comparison failed, and the branch took `else` on every event for the life of the workflow. Hookie now **refuses a branch whose condition path cannot resolve**, and the error names the keys that do exist. A path pointing at a key an earlier step has not written yet is refused for the same reason.
</Warning>

Branch sub-steps run _synchronously_: every step type except `delay` and `wait_for_event`, which cannot sit inside a branch; put them after the branch instead. This keeps a branch's execution atomic and its outcome immediately visible on the graph.

### Branching on an AI answer

`gt`, `gte`, `lt` and `lte` compare numbers, and a model replies with text, so what a `call_ai` or `agent_call` step puts in the context matters. A reply that _is_ a JSON value is stored as that value; anything else is stored as the text the model wrote. So `"8"` becomes the number `8` and `{"score": 8, "reason": "…"}` becomes an object, while `"Looks like a good fit"` stays a string.

Models do not always answer with bare JSON. A reply wrapped in a ```` ```json ```` fence, or introduced with a line such as "Here is the JSON:", is stored as the JSON inside it: the first fenced block that parses, otherwise the first `{…}` or `[…]` in the text that parses. A number inside a sentence ("8 out of 10") stays text. The run's step history says what each reply became (`output_type`), and flags `json_parse_failed` when a reply had a fence or a brace but no JSON could be read from it — check there first when a branch on an AI answer always takes `else`.

That gives you two shapes that work, and it is worth picking one deliberately:

- **Ask for the bare value**: "reply with the number only" with `"output_key": "score"`, then branch on `{ "path": "score", "op": "gt", "value": 7 }`. That is the example above.
- **Ask for an object**: "reply with JSON: score and reason" with `"output_key": "ai"`, then branch on `{ "path": "ai.score", "op": "gt", "value": 7 }`. Conditions take dotted paths, so the reason travels with the score and both are visible in the instance context.

What does _not_ work is asking for JSON and then branching on the `output_key` itself: `path: "score"` would resolve to the whole object, and a numeric comparison against an object is false. That one is still accepted, because the path resolves; it is the _value_ that cannot compare. The instance context is shown in the run's details, so check there first when a branch always goes the same way. Secret-looking values (credential-named keys, `Bearer …`/`Basic …` values and URL passwords) are shown as `[redacted]` there, in the step samples, over MCP and in the workspace export. The run itself always uses the real values.

`output_dataset` is unaffected by any of this: it always records the model's reply as the text it actually wrote, so the audit trail is what was said, not what was inferred from it.

## Conditions

Every place Hookie takes conditions uses one shape and the same ten operators: routing rules, endpoint criteria, workflow entry conditions, `branch` and `wait_for_event` conditions, WebSocket triggers and AI triggers.

```json
{ "path": "status", "op": "equals", "value": "paid" }
{ "path": "status", "equals": "paid" }
```

The second line is shorthand for the first. Every condition in a list must hold, and an empty list matches everything. A list holds at most 50 conditions.

| Operator | Value | Matches when the field… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `equals` | a string, number, boolean or `null` | equals the value. |
| `not_equals` | a string, number, boolean or `null` | does not equal the value. A missing field counts as not equal. |
| `contains` | a string | is text that contains the value. |
| `exists` | none | is present and not `null`. |
| `gt` · `gte` | a number | is a number greater than · at least the value. |
| `lt` · `lte` | a number | is a number less than · at most the value. |
| `in` | a list of 1 to 100 strings, numbers, booleans or `null` | equals one of the values. |
| `regex` | a pattern | is text the pattern matches. See [Regular expressions](#regular-expressions). |

```json
{ "path": "amount", "op": "gte", "value": 100 }
{ "path": "country", "op": "in", "value": ["US", "CA", "MX"] }
{ "path": "email", "op": "regex", "value": "@example\\.com$" }
```

- **Where equality is exact, and where it is text.** In workflows and triggers, `equals`, `not_equals` and `in` compare exactly, type included: `5` and `"5"` are different values. In rules and endpoint criteria they compare as text, as rules always have, so `5` and `"5"` are equal.
- **Numbers stay numbers.** `gt`, `gte`, `lt` and `lte` match only a field that is a number. A feed that sends a price as text (`"60000"`) never matches one.
- **Text stays text.** `contains` and `regex` match only a field that is a string.

A condition that could never match is refused when you save it, with a `400` that names the condition, rather than stored and silently never matching. That covers an unknown operator, a condition missing its value, both the shorthand and `op`/`value` at once (such as `{"path": "status", "op": "equals", "equals": "paid"}`), a key Hookie does not know, a numeric operator against anything but a number, `contains` or `regex` against anything but a string, an `in` without a list of 1 to 100 plain values, and a pattern outside the bounds below.

A condition's value can be a `{{placeholder}}` only in a `branch` or `wait_for_event`, where there is an instance context to read it from. Anywhere else it would be compared as the literal text `{{…}}`, so it is refused. The values in an `in` list are never resolved, so a placeholder there is refused too.

### Regular expressions

A `regex` value is a JavaScript regular expression, written without the surrounding slashes. It matches anywhere in the field unless you anchor it with `^` or `$`, and it is case-sensitive, so use a class such as `[Pp]aid` to match either case.

Patterns run against every matching event on a shared service, so each one is bounded to keep a single event cheap:

- **At most 200 characters**, and the pattern must compile.
- **No backreferences**, such as `\1` or `\k<name>`.
- **No nested quantifiers.** A repeated group that itself repeats or alternates, such as `(a+)+`, `(a|b)*` or `(\w+\s?)*`, is refused.
- **At most 2 open-ended repeats**: `*`, `+`, `{n,}`, or `{n,m}` with `m` above 16. `.*paid.*usd` is accepted; `.*a.*b.*c` is not.
- **Only the first 256 characters** of the field are tested.

A pattern that breaks a bound is refused when you save it. A stored pattern that breaks one (saved before these bounds existed) never matches; it is not run.

## Defining a workflow

Build workflows on the canvas under **Project → Workflows**, or send the definition to the API. A workflow's menu also has **Rename…** and **Activate** or **Deactivate**, which change nothing else and keep its version. Over the API, `PATCH /admin/api/projects/{pid}/workflows/{id}` merges what you send into the stored definition, so `{"active": false}` alone switches a workflow off, while `PUT` replaces the whole definition. The `update_workflow` tool and `hookie workflows update` send the whole definition. Either way it is this JSON; the builder's **Definition (JSON)** panel shows it for the workflow you are editing. Entry conditions use the shared condition shape; an empty array matches every record in the dataset.

```json
{
  "name": "Lead qualification",
  "entry_dataset": "leads",
  "entry_conditions": [
    { "path": "tier", "op": "equals", "value": "enterprise" }
  ],
  "steps": [
    { "type": "call_ai",
      "instructions": "Score this lead 1-10 for ICP fit. Reply with the number only.",
      "output_key": "score", "output_dataset": "lead_qualification" },
    { "type": "branch",
      "conditions": [{ "path": "score", "op": "gt", "value": 7 }],
      "then": [
        { "type": "emit_event", "dataset": "hot_leads", "payload": { "priority": "high" } }
      ],
      "else": [
        { "type": "emit_event", "dataset": "nurture_leads" }
      ] },
    { "type": "wait_for_event",
      "dataset": "approvals",
      "conditions": [
        { "path": "lead_id", "op": "equals", "value": "{{entry.id}}" },
        { "path": "status", "op": "equals", "value": "approved" }
      ],
      "timeout_seconds": 86400,
      "on_timeout": "continue" },
    { "type": "branch",
      "conditions": [{ "path": "waited_event", "op": "exists" }],
      "then": [
        { "type": "emit_event", "dataset": "approved_leads",
          "payload": { "approved_by": "{{waited_event.approver}}", "score": "{{score}}" } }
      ],
      "else": [
        { "type": "emit_event", "dataset": "escalations", "payload": { "reason": "no approval in 24h" } }
      ] },
    { "type": "delay", "seconds": 3600 }
  ]
}
```

## Step types

| Type | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `emit_event` | Write a record into a dataset and enqueue signed deliveries for it. The record is the entry record's fields with an optional `payload` merged over them, and the payload is [templated](#templating) from the instance context, so `"summary": "{{triage.summary}}"` delivers what an AI step produced. An emitted record is delivered, streamed and offered to the project's AI triggers like any other, and it is written together with its fan-out, so a fan-out interrupted part-way is finished later rather than lost. It never re-triggers workflow entry, so there are no feedback loops. With `"deliver": false` the record is only stored and streamed. |
| `call_ai` | Run an AI prompt over the instance context. The reply lands in the context under output_key (default ai_result), as a number or object if the model replied with one, so a later branch can compare it, and, verbatim as text, in output_dataset. The output_dataset record is delivered like any other record: to the project's destinations whose filter admits the dataset, the live stream and AI triggers. Like an emitted record, it never starts a workflow, not even one that starts on that dataset. |
| `agent_call` | Invoke a reusable AI agent by ID: its model, system prompt, and memory dataset apply. The result lands under output_key (default agent_result), interpreted the same way call_ai's is. In the builder, pick the agent from a list. |
| `http` | Call an https URL on a public host with `method` (GET, POST, PUT, PATCH or DELETE), optional `headers` (up to 32) and a JSON `body` (up to 64 KB; not on GET). A `{{path}}` placeholder can appear after the host. The response lands in the context under output_key (default http_result). `timeout_seconds` is 1 to 30. |
| `set` | Write `values` into the context. A value of exactly `"{{entry.email}}"` copies that context path; anything else is stored as written. |
| `transform` | Build a new object from context paths: each field in `mapping` takes the value at its path. The object lands under output_key (default transformed). |
| `branch` | Conditional if/then/else. Evaluate conditions against the instance context and run the matching then or else sub-sequence, including branching on an earlier call_ai or agent_call result. Branches can nest up to 3 levels deep. |
| `wait_for_event` | Suspend until a record matching the conditions arrives in a dataset. The conditions read the arriving record, and a value can come from this run, as in `"{{entry.order_id}}"` (see [Waiting for this run's record](#waiting-for-this-runs-record)). An optional `timeout_seconds` sets how long to wait. By default a timeout moves the instance to `timed_out`. With `"on_timeout": "continue"`, the run carries on with `waited_event` set to `null`. |
| `delay` | Suspend for N seconds before the next step, accurate to the second. |

## Waiting for this run's record

A `wait_for_event` condition's value can name the run's own context. Hookie resolves it once, when the run starts waiting, so the wait matches the record that belongs to this run and no other:

```json
{ "type": "wait_for_event",
  "dataset": "approvals",
  "conditions": [
    { "path": "order_id", "op": "equals", "value": "{{entry.order_id}}" },
    { "path": "status", "equals": "approved" }
  ],
  "timeout_seconds": 86400,
  "on_timeout": "continue" }
```

Without the first condition, every run waiting on `approvals` would take the first approval that arrived, whichever order it was for. With it, an approval for order `B2` resumes the run that started from order `B2` and leaves the others waiting.

The placeholder has to be something the run has at that point: the entry record (`entry.…`) or a key an earlier step wrote. A placeholder that could never resolve is refused when you save. If the value is empty for a particular run (that entry record has no `order_id`), the run fails when it reaches the wait, rather than waiting for any record at all.

### When nothing arrives

`"on_timeout": "continue"` turns a timeout into an answer. The run carries on with `waited_event` set to `null`, so a `branch` on `{ "path": "waited_event", "op": "exists" }` takes `then` when the record came and `else` when it did not — "nobody approved in 24 hours, so escalate". Without `on_timeout`, or with `"fail"`, a timeout ends the run as `timed_out`, as before.

## AI agents

An **AI agent** is a reusable, named LLM persona: a model, a system prompt, a bounded token cap, and an optional _memory dataset_ that every output is also written to for recall. Define agents next to your workflows in the console, then reference one from any workflow with an `agent_call` step, including inside a branch. Update the agent once; every workflow that uses it picks up the change.

**Deactivate** an agent from its menu to switch it off: its definition is kept, a workflow step that names it fails until you **Reactivate** it, and nothing else changes. **Delete** removes it for good, after a warning; a step that names it then fails with not found. Over the API, `DELETE /admin/api/projects/{pid}/ai-agents/{id}` only retires the agent, as the `delete_ai_agent` tool does, and `?permanent=true` removes it.

## Templating

`emit_event` payloads, `http`, `set` and `transform` read the instance context with `{{path}}`, and so do the values of `branch` and `wait_for_event` conditions. A value that is exactly `"{{entry.email}}"` keeps its type (a number stays a number, an object stays an object). Inside longer text, as in `"https://api.example.com/users/{{entry.id}}"` or `"Ticket {{entry.id}}: {{triage.summary}}"`, the value is inserted as text; in a URL it is also URL-encoded, and it may only appear after the host.

In an `emit_event` payload, a placeholder that resolves to nothing for a run leaves that field out of the emitted record. A placeholder whose first part is not something the run can have (`entry`, `waited_event`, or a key an earlier step writes) is refused when you save, and the error lists the keys that do exist.

## Durable execution

Workflows run on [Cloudflare Workflows](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workflows/). Each step is durable: a step that fails on a transient error is retried, a run survives restarts, and `delay` and `wait_for_event` suspend the run without holding anything open. A step that already completed is never run twice, so a retried run does not emit the same event again. An AI step is the exception: re-running it calls the model again, and counts against your AI allowance again.

## Instances & observability

Each matching entry record starts an **instance** (a run). The console lists instances per workflow with a drill-down into the live context and a per-step history, and shows any run on the workflow's graph (see [Runs on the graph](#runs-on-the-graph)), so you can see exactly where a flow is and why it stopped. A running or waiting instance can be **cancelled**, and a failed, timed-out or cancelled one **retried** from the start, from the console, the API (`POST /admin/api/projects/{pid}/workflow-instances/{id}/cancel` and `…/retry`), the CLI (`hookie workflows cancel|retry`) or a connected agent. Over the API, `GET /admin/api/projects/{pid}/workflows/{id}/instances` takes `state`, `since`, `until`, `limit` and `offset`, and answers with a `total`.

A run's context starts as a copy of the record that started it, so run history is kept for your plan's retention window, like the records themselves: a finished run (completed, failed, cancelled or timed out) is removed, with its step history, once both its start and its last change are older than the window. A run that is pending, running or waiting is never removed.

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `pending` | Created from a matching entry record; about to run. |
| `running` | Executing steps. |
| `waiting` | Suspended on a delay or wait_for_event. |
| `completed` | Every step finished. |
| `failed` | A step errored; last_error records why. |
| `timed_out` | A wait_for_event passed its deadline, and its `on_timeout` is not `continue`. |
| `cancelled` | Stopped with **Cancel run**, or from the API, the CLI or a connected agent. |

## Limits

- Up to **32 steps** per workflow (branch sub-steps count toward the total).
- Branch nesting depth: **3** levels.
- Active-instance cap per plan: **25** on Free, **500** on Pro, **2,000** on Team.
- `delay` and `wait_for_event` timeouts are exact to the second, up to **365 days**. A wait with no timeout gives up after 365 days.
- A definition's steps are at most **256 KiB** of JSON, and an instance's context at most **768 KiB**. `set` writes at most 64 keys.
- AI steps are bounded to 1,024 output tokens and 8,000 characters of instructions per step.

## Start triggers

A workflow starts when a record lands in its entry dataset. Besides webhooks, two kinds of trigger land records on their own. Both are under **Start triggers** on the Workflows tab, and owners and admins can create them.

### Cron triggers

A cron trigger lands one record on a schedule: a 5-field cron expression (`minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week`) read in the trigger's **time zone**. `0 9 * * 1-5` in `America/New_York` fires at 09:00 New York time on weekdays, on both sides of a daylight-saving change. Give the zone as an IANA name. The console fills in your browser's zone, and the API defaults to `UTC`.

- The scheduler runs every 5 minutes, so a schedule whose firings are closer than that is refused. `*/5 * * * *` is the finest one accepted.
- At a daylight-saving change, a local time that happens twice fires once, at its first occurrence. A local time the change skips (02:30 on the night clocks go from 02:00 to 03:00) fires just after the gap instead of being skipped.
- Creating a trigger returns its next three fire times. To see them before saving, send `POST /admin/api/projects/{pid}/cron-triggers/preview` with `schedule` and `timezone`.
- **Edit** changes the name, schedule, time zone, payload or dataset. A new schedule or time zone moves the next run. Resuming a paused trigger also moves it, so a trigger does not fire the moment it is resumed for a time that passed while it was paused.

### WebSocket triggers

A WebSocket trigger holds a connection to a feed open and lands a record for every frame that matches its [conditions](#conditions). The conditions are checked when you save, so a misspelled operator or a `gt` against text is an error there. Before this change, the trigger was saved, reported **connected**, and never matched a frame. `gt`, `gte`, `lt` and `lte` compare numbers, so a feed that sends prices as text (`"60000"`) never matches one.

**Mappings** reshape each matching frame the way a rule's mappings do: each `{ "path": "data.p", "key": "price" }` picks a field out of the frame into a key of the event. With no mappings, the frame is kept as it arrived. The raw submission always keeps the frame exactly as received; the mapped event is what is filed into the trigger's dataset, or handed to the project's rules when the trigger names no dataset. A mapping key must be a plain identifier (a letter, then letters, digits or underscores); anything else is refused with `400` when you create the trigger, rather than saved and then dropping every frame. Set mappings over the API (`POST /admin/api/projects/{pid}/ws-triggers`), with a connected agent's `create_ws_trigger`, or with `hookie ws-triggers create --mappings`.

- **Edit** changes the name, URL, connect message, conditions or dataset, each checked as it is when you create the trigger. A new URL, connect message, set of conditions or dataset reconnects the socket; a new name does not. Over the API, `PATCH /admin/api/projects/{pid}/ws-triggers/{id}` also takes `headers` and `subprotocols`.
- The URL, headers and connect message are stored encrypted and never shown again, so the Edit form leaves the URL blank: leave it blank to keep the current connection. A new URL is saved with the connect message you enter, and none if you leave it blank.
- Moving the URL to a **different host** is refused unless the request also sends each stored connection header and connect message (`null` for none). They often carry the feed's credentials, and Hookie will not pass them to a new host that nobody chose to give them to.

### AI triggers

An AI trigger runs an AI agent when a matching record lands. Its reply is stored as a record in the trigger's output dataset, and that record is fanned out like any other: delivered to the project's destinations whose filter admits the dataset (counted against the delivery allowance), and sent to the live stream. It carries the hop count of the event that triggered it. It never fires an AI trigger and never starts a workflow, so a trigger that matches its own output cannot loop.

If the model is briefly unavailable (busy, rate-limited, timing out, or failing on its side), the run is retried after about 30 seconds, then 2 minutes, then 10 minutes, and marked failed with the model's error after the fourth attempt. While it waits, the run shows `queued` and says why. A request the model refuses (for example, an unknown model) fails at once. A run refused because the month's AI allowance is used up also fails at once and is not retried.

## AI allowance and the AI call log

Every AI call counts the tokens it sends to the model and the tokens the model answers with against your plan's **monthly AI token allowance**. That includes a `call_ai` step, an `agent_call` step and an AI trigger run.

| Plan | AI tokens / month |
|---|---|
| Free | 100,000 |
| Pro | 750,000 |
| Team | 1,500,000 |

A typical classification step uses about 1,800 tokens. The largest a single step can use is about 4,600.

Once the month's allowance is used, the next AI call is **refused before the model runs**. The step fails with the reason, for example *Monthly AI allowance used: 750,000 of 750,000 tokens on the Pro plan for 2026-09*, and so does the workflow instance or trigger run. AI steps work again on the 1st (UTC), or straight away after an upgrade. **Home** shows this month's usage against the allowance, with a warning from 80%.

Every call is logged in the project's **Observability → AI calls**. Each entry records:
- the step, agent or trigger that made the call, and the model;
- the instructions, the input and the model's output, each kept up to 8,000 characters and marked when cut;
- the tokens in and out, the latency, and whether the call succeeded, failed or was refused.

Search matches the text of the instructions, input or output. The log is kept for your plan's retention window, and the audit log gets a daily summary of each day's AI usage.

Workflows are built in the console. [Sign in](https://app.hookie.ai/) and open a project's **Workflows** tab. For end-to-end patterns, see the [use cases](https://hookie.ai/use-cases).
