The **customer portal** is an embeddable, token-authed surface your _end customers_ use to manage their own destinations, the URLs your webhooks are delivered to, and inspect deliveries, so "ship webhooks to our customers" stops generating support tickets. No separate auth backend: you issue an access token per customer, and the destinations a token can see and manage are that customer's own.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up the project's portal">
    Each project has exactly **one** customer portal. In the console, open the project's **Settings → Customer portal**, switch it on, give it a name, and pick which of the project's datasets it exposes. Those datasets are the boundary of what the portal reaches: customers' destinations receive them and nothing else.

    The boundary is checked on every event, against the portal as it is now. Stop exposing a dataset and it stops arriving at every customer destination, including ones created before the change; a delivery already queued for it is cancelled rather than sent. A portal that exposes **no** datasets delivers nothing, and customers cannot add a destination to it. A portal that is switched off delivers nothing and refuses its tokens.

    Optionally, set your [branding](#branding) and the [sites allowed to embed the portal](#embed-it-in-your-app).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Issue a customer token">
    In the same pane, under **Access tokens**, issue one per customer using your own ID for them, and optionally a **customer name**. You get a one-time link:

    ```http
    https://app.hookie.ai/portal/{workspace}/{portal-slug}#token={token}
    ```

    The token (`hpt_…`) is shown once and stored hashed. It sits in the URL **fragment** (after `#`), which a browser never sends to a server, so it stays out of request logs. Tokens issued from the console expire after 30 days unless you change that when issuing; revoke from the same list at any time. Issuing and revoking a token are both recorded in **Settings → Audit log**.

    With a customer name (up to 80 characters), the portal says **Signed in as {name}**. Without one it names nobody. It never shows your customer ID.

    Links issued before this change carry `?token=…` instead. They still open the portal.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Share or embed">
    Send the link directly, or [embed the portal](#embed-it-in-your-app) in your own app.

    On first load the portal removes the token from the address bar and trades it for a **session**: an HttpOnly cookie that lasts 8 hours, or until the token expires if that is sooner. The token is not stored in the browser. Revoking the token ends its sessions at once. **Sign out** clears the session. If the browser blocks the cookie (for example, third-party cookies are blocked in an embedded frame), the portal keeps the token in memory for that page only.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What your customers can do

Everything is scoped to the customer's token: your workspace, the project, the portal and their customer ID.

- **See what they will receive.** Each exposed dataset is listed with an **example payload**: the envelope Hookie sends, around the shape of that dataset's latest record with every value replaced (strings become `"<string>"`, numbers `0`, booleans `false`). A portal's customers share its datasets, so a real value from another customer's event never appears, only field names.
- **Verify signatures.** A guide to checking either signature every delivery carries (see [Verifying deliveries](#verifying-deliveries) below).
- **Manage their endpoints.** Add one for chosen event types (HTTPS only, within the exposed datasets), edit its name, URL and event types, pause and resume it, and delete it. The event types must be a non-empty subset of what the portal exposes; an empty list is refused rather than read as "all".
- **Manage signing secrets.** Show an endpoint's signing secret again, and **rotate** it. The old secret keeps signing alongside the new one for 24 hours so their receiver can move over, and they can end that overlap early.
- **Read their own data in tables you design.** Any [saved view](#share-a-table-with-your-customers) you share appears as a read-only **Your data** table, with a picker when you share more than one, paged 25 rows at a time. They see only the columns you chose and only the rows that belong to them.
- **Inspect deliveries.** The delivery log shows the destination, status, response code, error, the next retry and a preview of the first 300 characters of what their endpoint answered. Open a delivery for its full response, endpoint URL and content type.
- **Replay a delivery.** A replay is a new delivery, charged to **your** monthly delivery allowance like a replay from the console. It is refused, not held, when the allowance is used up.

Every change a customer makes, and every time they reveal a signing secret, is recorded in **Settings → Audit log** with `portal|{customer_id}` as the actor.

## Share a table with your customers

A saved view of a dataset (see [Datasets and records](/datasets-and-records)) can be shared in the portal as part of your service: an order history, a list of tickets, a usage report.

1. Open the dataset in the console, then create or edit a view. Pick the columns, their headings and the sort as usual.
2. Tick **Share in the customer portal** and choose the **customer id field**, the field in your records that holds the same customer ID you use when issuing a portal token (a dotted path such as `customer.id` reads a nested field).
3. Save. Customers see the table the next time they open the portal. Clear the checkbox to stop sharing; the view stays in your console.

A customer sees a row only when the customer id field equals their own ID, and only the columns of the view; the record ID and every other field stay private. The table is read-only, and a view with no customer id field is never shown in the portal, so there is no way to share a view with every customer unfiltered. Records whose customer id field is missing are shown to nobody.

Over the API, set `portal_customer_key` when you [create or update a record view](/api-reference) (agents can pass the same field to `create_record_view`). Customers read the tables with `GET /portal/api/views` and `GET /portal/api/views/{id}/records`, which take the same portal token or session as the rest of the portal API.

## Verifying deliveries

The portal's **Example payloads** section ends with a verification guide. Every delivery is signed twice with the endpoint's signing secret (`whsec_…`), both over the same timestamp, so your customers can check either signature.

### Option 1: Hookie-Signature

The `Hookie-Signature` header looks like `t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256>`. Compute an HMAC-SHA256 of `<t>.<raw request body>`, using the whole `whsec_…` secret as the key. Accept the request when any `v1` matches and `t` is recent. During a secret rotation the header carries one `v1` for each live secret, for 24 hours. The portal shows a Node.js snippet, and [Verify signatures](/signatures) has more languages.

### Option 2: Standard Webhooks

Every delivery also carries the [Standard Webhooks](https://www.standardwebhooks.com) headers `webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp` and `webhook-signature`. Rather than writing the check themselves, your customers can use an [official `standardwebhooks` library](https://github.com/standard-webhooks/standard-webhooks/tree/main/libraries). There are libraries for JavaScript, Python, Go, Ruby, PHP, Java, Rust, C# and more.

Pass the `whsec_` signing secret to the library **exactly as shown**. Don't strip the prefix or base64-decode it; the library does both.

```js
import { Webhook } from "standardwebhooks";

const wh = new Webhook(secret); // the whsec_… secret, as is
const event = wh.verify(rawBody, {
  "webhook-id": req.headers["webhook-id"],
  "webhook-timestamp": req.headers["webhook-timestamp"],
  "webhook-signature": req.headers["webhook-signature"],
});
```

- **Signature format.** `webhook-signature` is `v1,<base64 HMAC-SHA256>` over `<webhook-id>.<webhook-timestamp>.<raw request body>`. During a rotation it carries one space-separated `v1,` value for each live secret.
- **Retries.** `webhook-id` is the delivery id, so a retry repeats it. To ignore an event you've already processed, deduplicate on `Hookie-Event-Id`.

`GET /portal/api/examples` returns both schemes: `signature` for Hookie-Signature, and `standard_webhooks` for the Standard Webhooks set.

## Branding

Under **Settings → Customer portal**, set:

- **Logo URL**: an `https` image URL, shown in the portal's header.
- **Primary colour**: a `#rrggbb` colour for the portal's buttons and focus ring. Button labels switch between black and white, whichever stays readable on it.

Anything else is refused with `400`. There is no free-form CSS.

## Embed it in your app

Browsers show the portal inside another site's `iframe` only when that site is on the portal's allowlist. The portal sends `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors` with exactly that list, and `'none'` when the list is empty.

<Warning>
  Embedding is **off** until you list your site. If you were already embedding the portal before this change, add your site's origin under **Sites allowed to embed the portal**, or browsers will refuse to show the frame. Direct links are unaffected.
</Warning>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Allow your site">
    Under **Settings → Customer portal → Sites allowed to embed the portal**, enter one origin per line, up to 10, such as `https://app.example.com`. Each must be an exact `https` origin: no path and no wildcard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the frame">
    Copy the snippet from **Embed the portal** in the same pane. It looks like this:

    ```html
    <iframe id="hookie-portal" title="Webhook settings" style="width:100%;border:0" height="800"
      src="https://app.hookie.ai/portal/{workspace}/{portal-slug}#token=ACCESS_TOKEN"></iframe>
    <script>
      window.addEventListener("message", (e) => {
        if (e.origin !== "https://app.hookie.ai" || e.data?.source !== "hookie-portal") return;
        const frame = document.getElementById("hookie-portal");
        if (e.data.type === "resize") frame.style.height = e.data.height + "px";
        if (e.data.type === "expired") { /* fetch a fresh link from your server and set frame.src */ }
      });
    </script>
    ```

    Issue `ACCESS_TOKEN` from your server (`POST /admin/api/projects/{project_id}/portals/{id}/tokens`) for the signed-in customer. Never put a token in page source.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The portal posts three messages to the page that embeds it, and only to the origins you allowed:

| Message | When |
| --- | --- |
| `{ source: "hookie-portal", type: "ready" }` | The portal has loaded. |
| `{ source: "hookie-portal", type: "resize", height }` | Its content changed size. Set the frame's height to fit. |
| `{ source: "hookie-portal", type: "expired" }` | The session or the token has ended. Load a fresh link. |

The messages carry only a type and a height, never a token or event data. The session cookie is `SameSite=None; Partitioned`, so it works inside your frame.

## Offboard a customer

Two ways to cut a customer off, both under **Access tokens**:

- **Revoke** a token. If it was the customer's last live token on the portal, their destinations are switched off in the same step.
- **Offboard customer** revokes every token they hold and switches off every destination they created, at once. It asks you to confirm first, and is recorded in the audit log as `offboard_portal_customer`.

Either way they stop receiving events. If you issue them a new token later, their destinations are still off, for them to turn back on.

In **Connections**, a customer's destination carries a **Portal customer · {name}** tag, so it never reads as one of your own. `GET /admin/api/projects/{project_id}/destinations` returns `portal_id`, `customer_id` and `customer_name` for them.

## Remove a portal

Switching a portal off is reversible: its tokens stop working and it delivers nothing, but everything comes back when you switch it on. To be done with a portal for good, open **Settings → Customer portal → Remove portal** and type the portal's name to confirm. Removing it:

- revokes every access link at once;
- cancels the deliveries still waiting to go to its customers' destinations (queued, retrying or paused), and sends those destinations nothing more;
- keeps the destinations and their delivery history, and deletes nothing;
- is recorded in **Settings → Audit log** as `remove_portal`, with how many tokens, destinations and deliveries it cut off.

It cannot be undone. The project can then have a new portal, with a new link. Removing a portal needs the developer role or above, and is refused while the workspace is suspended.

From the API it is `DELETE /admin/api/projects/{project_id}/portal` with `{"confirm": "<the portal's name>"}`. The name must match exactly, so a stray `DELETE` cannot remove a portal; without it the answer is `400`. A project with no portal, or one already removed, answers `404`. To switch a portal off instead, which you can undo, update it with `enabled: false` (or `hookie portals delete`, which only switches it off).

## Portal API

Everything the portal UI does is also available as a small token-authed API:

```http
# All requests: Authorization: Bearer <token>   (or the portal's session cookie)
POST   /portal/api/session                                   # trade the token for an 8-hour session cookie
DELETE /portal/api/session                                   # sign out (clears the cookie)
GET    /portal/api/me                                        # portal name + branding, customer name, event types
GET    /portal/api/event-types                               # datasets the portal exposes
GET    /portal/api/examples                                  # a redacted example per dataset + both signature schemes
GET    /portal/api/destinations                              # the customer's own destinations
POST   /portal/api/destinations                              # create one (https URL; signing secret shown once)
PATCH  /portal/api/destinations/{id}                         # name, url, dataset_filter and/or enabled
DELETE /portal/api/destinations/{id}                         # delete it
GET    /portal/api/destinations/{id}/secret                  # show its signing secret
POST   /portal/api/destinations/{id}/rotate-secret           # rotate, keeping the old secret for 24 hours
POST   /portal/api/destinations/{id}/expire-previous-secret  # end that overlap now
GET    /portal/api/deliveries                                # recent deliveries to the customer's destinations
GET    /portal/api/deliveries/{id}                           # one delivery in full
POST   /portal/api/deliveries/{id}/replay                    # send it again
```

`PUT` is accepted as well as `PATCH` on a destination. The token is not accepted in the query string (`?token=…`): a token there ends up in request logs. Each token can make about 60 calls a minute. That is a best-effort guard, not an exact count: each Cloudflare location counts separately, so a short burst can get through above it. Over it, the API answers `429` with `Retry-After: 60`. Writes made with the session cookie, rather than a bearer token, must send `X-Requested-With: fetch`.

| Status | When |
| --- | --- |
| `402` | Turning on a destination that is paused because your workspace is over its plan's limit, or adding one past that limit. Only you can upgrade, so the customer is told to contact you. |
| `409` | Replaying a delivery that is still being sent, whose destination is paused or deleted, or whose dataset the portal no longer exposes. Adding a destination to a portal that exposes no datasets. |
| `429` | Over the token's limit of about 60 calls a minute, or a replay when your monthly delivery allowance is used up (`"reason": "delivery_allowance_exhausted"`). |

<Note>
  Why one portal per project: a project is one customer's space. Two portals over the same project would show the same events to two audiences, so Hookie keeps it to one — creating a second is refused. Workspaces that had several portals in one project kept the most recently updated; the others were retired (tokens revoked, their destinations paused, nothing deleted), and each retirement is listed in **Settings → Audit log**.
</Note>

## Scoping guarantees

| Boundary | Guarantee |
| --- | --- |
| **Tenant + project** | A project has one portal, and a token is bound to it: the portal only ever reads that project's data. |
| **Customer** | A customer only ever sees and manages destinations and deliveries tagged with their own customer_id. Another customer's gets `404`. |
| **Datasets** | Customers' destinations receive only the datasets the portal exposes at the time of each event. Narrowing the portal narrows existing destinations too; a portal exposing none delivers nothing. |
| **Examples** | Built from field names only. Every value is replaced, so no customer sees another's data. |
| **Transport** | Destination URLs must be HTTPS; each gets its own HMAC signing secret, shown once and then only through the audited reveal. |
| **Tokens** | Stored hashed (SHA-256) and looked up by that hash, never recoverable; 30-day expiry by default from the console; revocable at any time, which also ends the token's sessions. Carried in the link's fragment, never the query string. |
| **Framing** | Only the sites you list may embed the portal; with none listed, no site can. |

Deliveries made to customer destinations behave exactly like every other Hookie delivery: HMAC-signed, retried with backoff, dead-lettered, and [verifiable](/signatures). [Sign in](https://app.hookie.ai/) and open a project's **Settings → Customer portal** to try it.
