Single sign-on (SAML or OIDC) lets people at your company's email domain sign in to Hookie through your identity provider. It is an add-on on the **Team** plan for **$149 a month**, billed on your Team subscription. The **Ultimate** plan includes it.

Directory Sync (SCIM), which adds and removes people automatically, is part of Team and needs no add-on.

## Set it up

1. **Add the add-on.** As the workspace owner, open **Settings → Billing** and choose **Add single sign-on**. It turns on when Stripe confirms the charge.
2. **Request it.** In **Settings → Sign-in & SSO**, choose **Request single sign-on**, tick **Single sign-on**, and list your email domains, for example `acme.com`. The Hookie team reviews each request, usually within one business day.
3. **Connect your identity provider.** Once the request is approved, choose **Set up single sign-on**. A setup portal walks your IT admin through your identity provider's settings and asks you to prove you own each domain.

## Signing in

Once the connection is active, people at your domains sign in from the usual sign-in page. They enter their work email, and Hookie sends them to your identity provider. Emailed codes, GitHub and Google no longer work for those addresses.

[Two-step sign-in](/two-step-sign-in) still applies on top of single sign-on if someone has an authenticator app, or if the workspace requires one.

## Removing the add-on

If you remove the add-on, or a payment fails, people keep signing in through your identity provider, so nobody is locked out. Changing the setup or requesting single sign-on again needs the add-on.

## API

- `POST` and `DELETE /admin/api/billing/sso-addon` add and remove the add-on. These are owner only, and never available to an agent.
- `GET /admin/api/sso/requests` reports `sso_addon`. Without the add-on, asking for `sso` or opening its setup answers `402` with `reason: "sso_addon_required"`.

Requesting and setting up single sign-on is done by a person in the console. Connected agents and API keys are refused.
