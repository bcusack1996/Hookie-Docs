Hookie is a webhook platform: it gives you a URL to receive events, somewhere structured to keep them, logic to act on them, and a signed, retrying delivery out the other side. Everything runs on Cloudflare Workers, D1, and Durable Objects.

## How an event moves through Hookie

<Steps>
  <Step title="It arrives">
    Post to an endpoint's URL, where the high-entropy slug in `app.hookie.ai/{workspace}/{project}/{webhook}` is the credential, or to `/v1/ingest/{key}` with an ingest key. JSON, form posts, and multipart all work, so an HTML `<form>` can point straight at an endpoint with no JavaScript and no backend of your own.

    Before anything is stored, the request passes the endpoint's IP allowlist, its rate limit, a duplicate check (an `Idempotency-Key`, or the delivery id your provider already sends), and your plan quota. Refused requests are logged in the endpoint's Activity, and never counted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It's captured, then routed">
    The raw body is stored as a **submission** exactly as it arrived, before anything reshapes it, so "no rule matched" is always distinguishable from "the event never arrived."

    Addressed requests store the payload whole into the dataset the URL names. A keyed request with no dataset in the path is the one shape that consults **rules**: each enabled rule tests its conditions against the payload and, where they hold, maps fields out into a **record**. Rules are not first-match: one event can land in several datasets at once. An event no rule matches is stored whole in the key's default dataset rather than dropped.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It fans out">
    A new record triggers every matching **destination** (an outbound webhook, signed HMAC-SHA256, retried with backoff), pushes onto the **live stream** for anyone tailing it, and starts any **workflow** whose trigger it satisfies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It gets acted on">
    A workflow runs steps in order: call a model, branch on the result, write to a dataset, emit a further event. Workflows also start on a schedule or from a WebSocket condition, not only from an inbound event.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Finding your way around the console

**Home** is the workspace at a glance. It charts events received, deliveries sent and AI runs over the last 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, each with its change on the period before. It also shows this month's usage against your plan, and a **Needs attention** list of anything that wants you: a failing or automatically disabled destination, or a limit you are close to. Its search box reaches any project, page or setting, and **Recently opened** lists the pages you visited last.

The side navigation groups an open project's pages under **Build** (endpoints, ingest keys, datasets, rules, workflows), **Deliver** (connections, deliveries) and **Observe** (observability, errors). Press <kbd>/</kbd> or <kbd>⌘K</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl+K</kbd> on Windows and Linux) to filter it as you type.
